# Visual language index

These references are an advisory vocabulary for describing visible qualities. Evidence remains authoritative: a style choice may change presentation, but never identity, plot facts, props, dialogue, or continuity locks.

A visual direction is used only when the request or `style_evidence` supports it. When no direction is supplied, present a small set of named options and wait for a selection.

| Dimension | Describe | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| medium | physical or digital image-making process | ink wash, oil paint, cinematic photography |
| palette | dominant hues and contrast relationship | charcoal and vermilion, muted earth colors |
| lighting | source, direction, hardness, atmosphere | soft window light, hard rim light, overcast diffusion |
| texture | visible surface and mark character | paper fibers, dry brush edges, fine film grain |
| composition | framing and spatial hierarchy | centered portrait, deep staging, generous negative space |
| camera feel | viewpoint and optical impression | intimate eye level, observational handheld feel |
| motion quality | speed, weight, path, response | deliberate turn, fabric trails with inertia |

Use [style directions](style-presets.md), [medium vocabulary](medium-glossary.md), and [motion vocabulary](motion-glossary.md) as optional wording sources. Record why each selected phrase is supported.
